This site collects very little. It does not ask you for data, build a profile of you, show ads, sell anything to anyone or store anything about you on your device. It only measures, in aggregate, how many people visit and how fast it loads. As on any site, whoever serves it receives your IP address. Here is what is processed, on what legal basis, and what you can ask of me.

## Data controller

- **Controller:** Juan José López Jiménez
- **Contact:** [hello@lojopez.dev](mailto:hello@lojopez.dev)
- **Full identification details:** they are in the [legal notice](/en/legal-notice).
- **Data protection officer:** none has been appointed. The volume and nature of the processing described here do not make it mandatory under article 37 of the [General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A02016R0679-20160504).

## What data is processed

- **If you email me.** The site has no forms. The only way to send me data is to write to [hello@lojopez.dev](mailto:hello@lojopez.dev) directly. In that case I process your email address, your name if you include it, and the content of the message, only to read it and reply. Writing to me is optional: if you do not, the site works the same.
- **Technical data of your visit.** Every website receives the data your browser sends to request a page: your IP address, the address of the page, the browser type and the time. On this site Cloudflare receives it, because it serves the site, and uses it to deliver the page and protect the service from attacks. Cloudflare's dashboard shows me some of it, such as the IP addresses with the most requests.
- **Aggregate statistics.** I use two Cloudflare tools. Domain Analytics shows traffic figures, such as requests, page views, visitors by country, errors, the most requested pages and the IP addresses with the most requests. Web Analytics measures how the site is used and how it performs, such as page views, visits, country, browser type and load times. According to Cloudflare, Web Analytics uses no cookies or local storage and does not identify individuals. Apart from the list of IP addresses with the most requests (see "[IP addresses](#ip-addresses)" below), I see aggregate figures in the dashboards.
- **Language.** The language is decided by the address of the page (`/es` or `/en`). If you arrive at the root of the site, the server reads the language your browser sends (the `Accept-Language` header) only to redirect you to `/es` or `/en`. I do not store it anywhere. For what the site stores in your browser, see the [cookie policy](/en/cookies).

## Third-party recommendations

The recommendations section shows the name, role, photo and text that each person published on their public LinkedIn profile, or a translation of it, with a link to that profile. I publish them so visitors can see what people I have worked with say about my work. If you are one of them and want something changed or removed, write to me.

## Automated decisions

This site makes no decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling.

## IP addresses

An IP address can identify a person, and I treat it as personal data. My site does not collect your IP address by itself. Cloudflare sees it anyway, because it could not deliver the page without it, and uses it for security and for its traffic statistics. Cloudflare's dashboard shows me the IP addresses that make the most requests, so I can see them too, but I do not know who each one belongs to.

## Legal basis

When you write to me, I process the message to reply, which is what you are asking for. The legal basis is legitimate interest under article 6(1)(f) of the [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A02016R0679-20160504), or pre-contractual steps under article 6(1)(b) if you are proposing a job.

Serving the page, security and aggregate statistics also rest on legitimate interest: keeping the site working, fast and protected. This includes using your IP address to deliver the page, protect it and count traffic, and seeing in the dashboard the IP addresses with the most requests to tell usual traffic from unusual traffic. That interest outweighs the impact on your privacy because the statistics are aggregate and leave nothing on your device, and because the IP address is used to serve and protect the page, not to build a profile of you.

The recommendations rest on my legitimate interest in showing my professional references. They are texts their authors already published openly on LinkedIn, and they can ask me at any time to change or remove them.

I do not ask for your consent because nothing described here depends on it. If that ever changes, I will ask before starting, and you will be able to withdraw it at any time.

## Who else processes this data

I rely on one provider to serve and measure the site, and another for email. Both process the data to provide their service to me.

**Cloudflare, Inc.** Hosts and serves the site, provides perimeter security and runs the Domain Analytics and Web Analytics statistics. For the data I see in my dashboards, it acts on my behalf. For running its own network, it follows its own policy. [Its privacy policy](https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/).

**Apple (iCloud Mail).** Runs the mailbox that receives emails sent to [hello@lojopez.dev](mailto:hello@lojopez.dev). [Its privacy policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/en-ww/).

## Transfers outside the European Economic Area

Cloudflare is a US company, so the data it processes may leave the European Economic Area. As it states in its [GDPR information](https://www.cloudflare.com/trust-hub/gdpr/), it relies on the [standard contractual clauses approved by the European Commission in Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A32021D0914) and, where applicable, on [Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/1795, which recognises an adequate level of protection for data transferred under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A32023D1795).

According to Apple's privacy policy, the entity that controls data in the European Economic Area is Apple Distribution International Limited, in Ireland. Because Apple belongs to a group based in the United States, the emails it handles may leave the European Economic Area. Apple states that these transfers rely on the standard contractual clauses of the European Commission.

## How long I keep the data

Emails stay in my inbox while the conversation still makes sense, and then I delete them. Cloudflare keeps the technical data and statistics for its own retention periods. It does not publish a single period: the criteria it uses are in its [privacy policy](https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/). The recommendations stay published until their author asks to change or remove them.

## Your rights

You can ask me for access to your data, to correct or delete it, to object to the processing, to restrict it, or to receive a portable copy of what you sent me. Write to [hello@lojopez.dev](mailto:hello@lojopez.dev) and I will reply within one month, as article 12 of the [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A02016R0679-20160504) requires.

In practice, this applies to emails you have sent me and to what is published in the recommendations section. The statistics are aggregate, so I cannot single out one person's data. From the IP address list I only see the addresses with the most requests and I do not know who each one belongs to, so I cannot check that one is yours.

If you think I have not handled your data properly, you can lodge a complaint with the [Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD)](https://www.aepd.es) or with the data protection authority of the country where you live.

## Changes to this policy

If I add a service that processes data differently from what is described here, I will update this page and the "last updated" date before turning it on.
